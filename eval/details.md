# Eval details

Measured on 2026-09-29 with `jev-1.13.0`, on the 40 hand-labeled postings in [`labels.csv`](labels.csv) and a full run of 571 postings. This is a snapshot written by hand. [`results.md`](results.md) is regenerated by `score.py` and lists every miss.

## PM accuracy

Of the 40 labeled postings, I marked 18 as PM roles and 22 as not. Jev and the title rule can also answer `adjacent`, meaning product-related work that isn't PM, like product marketing or product design. For this question, `adjacent` counts as not a PM role.

| My label | Jev: pm | Jev: adjacent | Jev: not_pm | Title rule: pm | Title rule: adjacent | Title rule: not_pm |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pm (18) | 18 | 0 | 0 | 17 | 0 | 1 |
| not_pm (22) | 0 | 20 | 2 | 0 | 18 | 4 |

Jev got 40 of 40. It found all 18 PM roles and didn't call any other role PM.

The title rule got 39 of 40. It missed "Director of Product, Growth/AI" at Brex.

If every option has to match exactly, Jev scores 20 of 40 and the title rule 21 of 40. Both scores are low because I never used `adjacent` when labeling, while both methods answered `adjacent` for most non-PM roles.

## AI accuracy

This is scored on the 18 labeled PM roles. I labeled 15 as AI product work and 3 as unclear, and the 3 unclear ones are left out.

| My label | Jev: ai_product | Jev: ai_feature | Jev: not_ai | Title rule: AI | Title rule: not AI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ai_product (15) | 9 | 6 | 0 | 5 | 10 |
| unclear (3) | 0 | 0 | 3 | 0 | 3 |

Jev got 15 of 15, counting both `ai_product` and `ai_feature` as AI work.

The title rule got 5 of 15. It called 10 AI roles non-AI because their titles don't mention AI, including "Staff Product Manager, Connect" at Stripe and "Senior Product Manager, Email Security" at Cloudflare.

On the exact label, Jev and I agree on 9 of 18. See Limits in the [README](../README.md#limits).

None of my 18 PM labels were `not_ai`. So this sample shows that Jev finds AI roles, but not whether it wrongly calls ordinary PM roles AI.

## ML background

This is also scored on the 18 labeled PM roles. A probability of 0.5 or more counts as "needs hands-on ML."

Jev agreed with me on 17 of 18. The one disagreement was "Staff Technical Product Manager" at Scale AI, which Jev scored 0.62 and I labeled no. The other 17 scored between 0.04 and 0.32.

None of the 18 roles needed hands-on ML by my labels. So the result shows that Jev rarely flags ML when it isn't needed, but not whether it can spot a role that does need it.

The title rule doesn't answer this question.

## Review rate

On the full run, 82 of 571 postings (14%) went to review. In 52 of them, the `role` answer was under its 0.7 floor. In the other 30, all PM roles, the `ai_focus` answer was under its 0.6 floor or `unclear`.

The other 489 postings were sorted into these buckets:

| Bucket | Postings |
|---|---|
| Not PM | 293 |
| AI PM | 91 |
| PM, not AI | 59 |
| PM on a product adding AI features | 42 |
| AI PM, needs hands-on ML | 4 |

## Cost

The full run made one call per posting, with all three questions in the same call.

| | |
|---|---|
| Total, 571 postings | $0.0459 |
| Per posting | about $0.00008 |
| Per 1,000 postings | about $0.08 |
| Input tokens | 1,092,206 total, 1,913 per posting on average (max 2,244) |
| Output tokens | 65,435 total |

Most of the cost is input tokens, since Jev reads the whole posting and the answers are short. Results are cached, so rerunning the same postings is free. A second run on a different company list cost $0.0174 for 209 new calls, about the same rate per posting.
